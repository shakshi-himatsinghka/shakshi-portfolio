Why I shoot on an iPhone

It's simpler, so I'm quicker. Nobody stiffens up when they see a phone the way they do in front of a big camera, so people stay themselves. And the footage feels raw, which is how a reel should feel.

The bits I'm always looking for

People who've forgotten I'm there. The laugh that turns into everyone laughing. The aunties on the dance floor. Two people having a quiet moment in the middle of all the noise.

I blend in

I'm not there to direct anyone. I keep to the side, work around your photographer, and film what's actually happening. You shouldn't have to think about me at all.

Good to know

Will an iPhone really look good enough?
Have a look at the reels on this page. For reels, a phone is often the better camera: it's quick, it's light, and people act like themselves in front of it.
Will you get in the way of our photographer?
No. I keep to the side and work around them. They do the big shots, and I catch the in-between bits.
What do I get at the end?
Edited reels, ready to post. Raw clips too, if we agree on them before the shoot.
How long does it take?
It depends on the event and how many reels you need. I'll give you a date before we start.
How many changes can I ask for?
Two rounds. Send your changes as one list each time, and I'll fix them.
How much does it cost?
It depends on how long the shoot is and how many reels you need, so I'll quote for your event.
Do you shoot outside Kolkata?
I'm open to it, but it depends on the event. Email me with the date and the place, and we'll work it out.
Can you just edit footage I already have?
Yes. Send me a Drive link, wherever you are.

a little backstory

How I started freelancing

Last year I was a marketing executive at a jewellery brand. A big part of my job was running influencer collaborations, so I was the point of contact for every creator we worked with.

For one store reel, I decided to edit it in-house instead of having the creator do it, because I had a specific direction in mind.

While we were coordinating, the creator asked me who had edited it. I said I had, and she really liked it. A while later she messaged me again, this time asking if I'd edit her own short-form videos.

That's how I landed my first freelance client. I wasn't looking for it; I was just doing my job, and the work found its way to the right person.

I've kept editing ever since. Most recently, I worked with Dipikka Poddaar as her talent manager and video editor, so I've seen a reel from both sides: what the brand needs from it, and what the creator needs it to feel like.
